## How Do You Run Product Backlog Grooming in monday.com?

You treat the backlog as a living, ranked board, not a static spreadsheet.

Backlog grooming (or refinement) is where most teams quietly lose sprint quality. If the backlog is messy, sprint planning becomes guesswork. monday.com fixes this with a dedicated backlog board that the product owner curates between sprints.

A solid backlog board in monday includes:

- **Title and user story**: written in the "As a [user], I want [outcome]" format.
- **Acceptance criteria**: a long-text or sub-item column so "done" is unambiguous.
- **Priority**: a status column with Critical, High, Medium, Low.
- **Effort**: a number column for story points or T-shirt sizes.
- **Sprint assignment**: a dropdown linking the item to a target sprint number.
- **Epic / Initiative**: a connect-boards column that ties items to a roadmap parent.

The [monday.com product development template](https://www.fruitionservices.io/post/monday-com-product-management-and-development) is built for this. As Fruition's product development guide notes, the template helps you stay on track with sprints, manage future and current sprints, assign owners, track execution, view priority, and prioritise backlogs.

Grooming itself becomes a recurring 60-minute event. The product owner walks the team through the top 15 to 20 backlog items, the team estimates effort, and you mark each item "Ready for Sprint" or "Needs more detail". Drag-and-drop reordering means priority is always visible to everyone, not buried in a tracker.

One workflow tip: use monday automations to ping the product owner when an item sits in "Needs more detail" for more than five days. That single recipe prevents the slow drift of stale stories.

## How Does Sprint Planning Work Inside monday?

Sprint planning becomes a 90-minute conversation, not a four-hour negotiation.

You start with the groomed backlog. You confirm team capacity for the next two weeks. You pull "Ready for Sprint" items into the sprint board until you hit capacity. That is it.

monday.com makes this fast because every item already has its effort, owner, and acceptance criteria attached. You can manage sprint planning and backlog grooming directly inside the platform, with a single click moving items from backlog to active sprint.

A few practical defaults that work well:

- **Two-week sprint cadence**: aligns with the industry standard. 59.1% of Scrum teams hold two-week sprints, according to Broadcom data.
- **Capacity buffer**: plan for 80% of theoretical capacity. Leave room for support work and meetings.
- **Sprint goal**: a single sentence pinned to the board describing what success looks like.
- **Definition of Done**: a checklist sub-item on every story, not a wiki page nobody reads.

The platform supports this naturally. The product development template gives you 30+ column types to customise workflows, multiple data visualisation views including Kanban, Timeline, Map, Workload, Gantt, and Calendar, customisable automations to avoid repetitive work, and integrations with third-party apps like Google Calendar, Zendesk, and Dropbox.

For teams running [cross-functional product collaboration across dev, design, and marketing](https://www.fruitionservices.io/post/cross-functional-product-collaboration-monday), the sprint board can also surface dependencies on adjacent teams. That visibility is what stops the classic "design wasn't ready" sprint failure.

## How Do You Track Velocity and Burndown in monday?

You build a dashboard once and never touch the maths again.

The metrics worth pinning to that dashboard:

## How Do You Run Sprint Reviews and Retrospectives?

You run them on the same boards you used to plan the sprint, which keeps the conversation grounded.

The sprint review is for stakeholders. You walk through completed items, demo working software, and accept or reject stories against the agreed criteria. The monday board is the demo script.

The retrospective is for the team. monday's product development template includes a structure to run sprint retrospective, get feedback and evaluate previous sprints to improve processes, and allow the team to vote on items to improve, discuss, or keep.

Why does the retrospective matter so much? Retrospectives are the most important agile event for introducing the agile mindset to teams, with the Scrum team reflecting on what went well and what went poorly at the end of each sprint. They are the core event for continuous improvement. If you adopt only one agile practice, let it be retrospectives. Everything else will follow.

A simple retro board structure in monday:

- **What worked**: green status, votes column.
- **What didn't**: red status, votes column.
- **What to try next sprint**: yellow status, owner column.
- **Action items**: linked to the next sprint board as committed work.

That last point matters. Retro action items that do not become real sprint work are theatre. monday's connect-boards column makes the link explicit.
