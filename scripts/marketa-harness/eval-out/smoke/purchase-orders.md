## Setting Up Your PO Board in monday.com

Start with the structure. A working PO board needs these columns as a baseline:

- **PO Number** (a unique identifier, auto-generated).
- **Vendor Name** (linked to a vendor master list).
- **Order Date** (when the PO was created).
- **Delivery Date** (expected arrival).
- **Order Amount** (total cost).
- **Approval Status** (stages: Draft, Pending Approval, Approved, Received, Invoiced, Closed).
- **Approver** (person responsible for sign-off).
- **Receipt Confirmation** (yes/no and date).
- **Notes** (for special instructions or vendor communication).

Next, configure your workflow stages. Your approval process might look like this: Draft → Pending Approval → Approved → In Transit → Received → Invoiced → Closed. Each stage triggers the next step automatically or alerts the right person to act.

Add conditional logic so that orders under £5,000 skip the finance approver and go straight to procurement sign-off. Orders over that threshold route to your finance team. This keeps high-value decisions with the right owner and keeps routine orders moving fast.

## Automations That Save Your Team Hours

Automation is where monday.com earns its place in your ops stack. Here are five automations most procurement teams implement first:

**Auto-assign POs to approvers.** When a new PO is created, the platform automatically assigns it to the right approver based on vendor or order amount. No one has to manually route it. No one wonders whose job it is to sign off.

**Notify vendors on approval.** When a PO moves to "Approved", trigger an email to the vendor with order details, delivery address, and payment terms. Your vendor has no excuse to say they didn't receive the order.

**Flag overdue deliveries.** If a delivery date passes and the order is still marked "In Transit", monday.com flags it automatically and notifies your operations manager. You catch delays before they become emergency calls.

**Sync receipt with invoice.** When an item is marked "Received", link it to the corresponding invoice in your accounting system (if you use an integration). This prevents duplicate payments and keeps finance and ops aligned.

**Weekly PO status report.** Set a weekly automation that sends your ops director a summary: how many POs are pending approval, how many are in transit, which vendors have open orders. One email replaces 30 minutes of manual status-checking.

## Integrating Vendors into Your PO Workflow

Your vendors don't use monday.com, so you need to decide how they interact with your orders.

**Email integration.** When a PO is approved, send the vendor a detailed email with all order specs. When they respond with a shipping date or tracking number, that information can be logged back into the PO board. This keeps your vendors in the loop without requiring them to have platform access.

**Portal access (optional).** If you have high-volume, strategic vendors, you can grant them limited access to a vendor portal within monday.com so they can see their open orders, delivery dates, and receipt status. This reduces back-and-forth email and improves vendor accountability.

**Integration with procurement tools.** If you already use a dedicated procurement platform, you can integrate it with monday.com via API or third-party connectors. This prevents data silos and keeps your PO truth in sync across systems.

## Tracking PO Performance and Spotting Bottlenecks

Once your PO workflow is live, use monday.com's reporting and dashboards to track performance.

**Approval cycle time.** How long does it take, on average, for a PO to move from Draft to Approved? If approvals are taking seven days, you have a bottleneck. Check if your approvers are overloaded or if your conditional logic is routing too many orders to one person.

**On-time delivery rate.** What percentage of orders arrive by the promised delivery date? If it's below 90%, it's time to have a conversation with your vendors or reconsider your lead times.

**Cost variance.** Are you paying what you quoted? Create a dashboard that shows budgeted vs. actual PO amounts. Large variances signal scope creep or vendor pricing changes you need to address.

**Vendor performance.** Which vendors have the shortest lead times, fewest quality issues, and best on-time delivery? Use this data to guide your supplier strategy and negotiate better terms with your best partners.

Monday.com's reporting tools let you build these dashboards without writing SQL. Slice the data by vendor, by month, by approver, or by cost centre. Share the dashboard with your finance team so everyone is working from the same facts.

## Common PO Management Challenges and How monday.com Addresses Them

**Challenge: Duplicate or lost orders.** When POs are scattered across email and spreadsheets, someone inevitably sends the same order twice or loses track of an approval request.

monday.com's centralised board is your single source of truth. Every PO has a unique record. Approvals are logged with timestamps. You eliminate duplicates and lost approvals by design.

**Challenge: Vendor miscommunication.** Vendors don't always know what you've ordered or when to deliver it. This creates delays and finger-pointing.

Automate vendor notifications so that the moment a PO is approved, your vendor gets a detailed email with all the information they need. Attach delivery instructions, payment terms, and contact details. Reduce back-and-forth by 80%.

**Challenge: Finance can't reconcile invoices.** When receipts and invoices don't align, your finance team spends days chasing discrepancies.

Link your PO receipt status to your invoice in monday.com. When an order is marked "Received", your finance team knows to expect an invoice from that vendor. Match invoice to receipt before payment. This three-way match (PO, receipt, invoice) is the gold standard for audit compliance.

**Challenge: Your team works across multiple systems.** Some team members use Slack, others use email, your finance team uses QuickBooks. Keeping everyone in sync is a nightmare.

Integrate monday.com with your existing tools (Slack notifications, email notifications, accounting software) so that PO updates travel to everyone, regardless of where they work. Your team's tools don't have to change; the updates just flow in.
